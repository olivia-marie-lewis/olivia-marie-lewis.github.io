excerpt: '<div style="display: flex; align-items: flex-start; gap: 30px;"><div style="width: 40%; flex-shrink: 0;"><img src= "{{"/images/Treatment_Drivetimes_112524.png" | relative_url }}" width="100%" style="border: 2px solid black;"></div><div style="width: 60%;">This report was part of a series to assess county-level vulnerability to opioid overdose, alcohol-related harms, and stimulant-related overdose across the state of Idaho, in collaboration with the Idaho Department of Health and Welfare. I produced descriptive maps of socioeconomic and health-related variables and used network analysis to visualize drive-time access to treatment, identifying gaps. I also assisted in building a statistical model to generate county-level vulnerability scores, providing an evidence base for targeted interventions. We ultimately found that many rural counties were highly vulnerable to alcohol-related harms and had long drive-times to treatment. Recommendations included increasing the availability of alternative transportation options, such as rideshare programs, to combat drinking and driving in remote areas, as well as reducing barriers to treatment.
